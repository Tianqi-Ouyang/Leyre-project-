Author

Tianqi Ouyang

Published

Overview

For every patient we compute the chemotherapy dose each estimated-GFR (eGFR) equation would prescribe and compare it to the dose that measured GFR (mGFR, ⁵¹Cr-EDTA clearance) — the gold standard — would prescribe. Each cell is the percent of patients mis-dosed in that subgroup:

  • OVERDOSE (%) — the equation would give more drug than mGFR warrants.
  • UNDERDOSE (%) — the equation would give less drug than mGFR warrants.

Traffic-light colour:  green <5%  ·  orange 5–10%  ·  red >10% .

Columns give the Overall rate and rates within Sex, BMI category, and Metastatic disease subgroups. Results are shown on both the indexed (mL/min/1.73 m²) and non-indexed (absolute mL/min) GFR scale; mGFR is always taken on the same scale as the equation. All 1,200 patients are used for every drug (a counterfactual — the file has no administered doses).

Drug Dosing rule
Cisplatin ≥60 full · 50–59 −25% · 40–49 −50% · <40 avoid
Methotrexate (CNS, 8 g/m²) ≥100 full · 40–99 reduced · <40 avoid
Melphalan (200 mg/m²) >60 full · 30–59 → 140 mg/m² · <30 forced reduce
Carboplatin (Calvert AUC 5) over/under at AUC 6 / AUC 4 (±20%) and the ±15% margin
Note

“Metastatic disease — Unknown” from the source template is omitted because metastatic status is complete (no missing) in this dataset. The creatinine equation shown is CKD-EPI 2021 (the race-free version present in the data).

5 Notes

  • Reference. Measured GFR (⁵¹Cr-EDTA) is the gold standard; each equation is compared to mGFR on the matching scale (indexed vs indexed, non-indexed vs non-indexed).
  • Scale. Calvert carboplatin dosing was derived on absolute (non-indexed) GFR (Section 4 is the clinically conventional view for carboplatin); indexed GFR (Section 3) is the conventional scale for CKD-staging cutoffs.
  • Direction. Creatinine-based equations tend to overdose (they overestimate GFR, especially at low BMI / low muscle mass); cystatin-based equations tend to underdose.
  • Counterfactual. No administered doses exist in this file, so every drug rule is applied to all 1,200 patients to estimate misclassification rates, not actual prescribing errors.
