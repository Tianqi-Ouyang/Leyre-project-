Chemotherapy Dosing: eGFR Equations vs Measured GFR (OncoGFR 1200)

Author

Tianqi Ouyang

Published

May 29, 2026

1 Setup

2 Data Management

Overview

For every patient we compute the chemotherapy dose each estimated-GFR (eGFR) equation would prescribe and compare it to the dose that measured GFR (mGFR, ⁵¹Cr-EDTA clearance) — the gold standard — would prescribe. Each cell is the percent of patients mis-dosed in that subgroup:

  • OVERDOSE (%) — the equation would give more drug than mGFR warrants.
  • UNDERDOSE (%) — the equation would give less drug than mGFR warrants.

Traffic-light colour — the number in each cell is the share of patients in that subgroup whose dose would be wrong in that direction (over- or under-dosed vs mGFR), coloured  green < 5 %  ·  orange 5–10 %  ·  red > 10 % . The 5 % / 10 % breaks are a chosen reporting convention (good / caution / concerning), not a fixed guideline.

Columns give the Overall rate and rates within Sex, BMI category, and Metastatic disease subgroups. Results are shown on both the indexed (mL/min/1.73 m²) and non-indexed (absolute mL/min) GFR scale; mGFR is always taken on the same scale as the equation. All 1,200 patients are used for every drug (a counterfactual — the file has no administered doses).

Dosing rules — one row per GFR band. Tier is the ordered dose code used internally (higher = more drug; 0 = avoid / forced reduce).

Drug GFR (mL/min) Dose Tier

40 – 49 −50 % 1
< 40 Avoid 0
Methotrexate (CNS, 8 g/m²) ≥ 100 Full dose 2

Carboplatin (Calvert) continuous (AUC) dose = target AUC × (GFR + 25), target AUC 5; over-/under-dose set by a ±20 % or ±15 % band — see note below —
NoteHow carboplatin over- and under-dosing are defined

Carboplatin is not dosed in GFR tiers like the other three drugs — it is dosed to a target drug exposure with the Calvert formula: dose (mg) = target AUC × (GFR + 25), with target AUC = 5. Because the dose is directly proportional to the GFR entered, plugging an eGFR equation into the formula instead of the true (measured) GFR changes the dose by the same percentage that the equation mis-estimates GFR. We flag only a clinically meaningful error, using a tolerance band around the AUC-5 target:

  • Overdose — the eGFR-based dose is larger than the dose from measured GFR at AUC 6. Since AUC 6 is 20 % above AUC 5, the equation would deliver > 20 % too much carboplatin.
  • Underdose — the eGFR-based dose is smaller than the dose from measured GFR at AUC 4 (20 % below AUC 5) → > 20 % too little.

That ±20 % band is reported as carboplatin_b20; carboplatin_b15 repeats the same test with a tighter ±15 % band (AUC 5.75 and 4.25). Both are shown because the analysis plan asked for 15 % and 20 % thresholds. (These definitions come from the eligibility-cutoffs document.)

Note

“Metastatic disease — Unknown” from the source template is omitted because metastatic status is complete (no missing) in this dataset. The creatinine equation shown is CKD-EPI 2021 (the race-free version present in the data).

Variable codebook

Every field used to build the tables. Inputs are columns read from OncoGFR1200_MGH_chemo_CJASN.xlsx (N = 1,200); derived fields are computed in this analysis.

Kidney-function inputs (eGFR / mGFR)

Each equation is stored twice — an index column (mL/min/1.73 m², BSA-normalised) and a non-index column (absolute mL/min ≈ indexed × BSA ⁄ 1.73). Measured GFR is the reference all equations are compared against; comparisons are made on the matching scale.

5 Notes

  • Reference. Measured GFR (⁵¹Cr-EDTA) is the gold standard; each equation is compared to mGFR on the matching scale (indexed vs indexed, non-indexed vs non-indexed).
  • Scale. Calvert carboplatin dosing was derived on absolute (non-indexed) GFR (Section 4 is the clinically conventional view for carboplatin); indexed GFR (Section 3) is the conventional scale for CKD-staging cutoffs.
  • Direction. Creatinine-based equations tend to overdose (they overestimate GFR, especially at low BMI / low muscle mass); cystatin-based equations tend to underdose.
  • Counterfactual. No administered doses exist in this file, so every drug rule is applied to all 1,200 patients to estimate misclassification rates, not actual prescribing errors.
